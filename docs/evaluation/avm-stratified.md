## Findings

1. **Comparable availability is the dominant error term.** With at least one comparable the median absolute percentage error is **11.09%**; with none it is **16.48%** — 1.5 times higher. COD moves from **15.38%** to **25.51%**, which takes the comparables-backed stratum closer to the IAAO residential acceptance band of 5-15%.
2. **The relationship is monotonic, not merely present.** Taking the subjects by exact comparable count from zero to four, COD falls 25.51% to 17.57% to 13.42% to 10.18% to 5.02% and PE25 rises 65.5% to 72.6% to 82.5% to 92.3% to 100.0%. Every additional like-for-like transaction improves both uniformity and accuracy, and at four comparables COD reaches **5.02%** — the strong end of the IAAO 5-15% band — on 7 subjects. The sample at four is small and the estimate correspondingly uncertain, but the direction across all five strata is unambiguous.
3. **What the comparables arm corrects is dispersion, not central tendency.** The two strata have almost the same median ratio (1.0445 with comparables against 1.0453 without), so the regression is not systematically over- or under-valuing on average. It is the spread that differs: MAPE of 27.05% against 16.79%, and a 90th-percentile error of 62.7% against 46.5%. The regression gets the middle of the distribution right and the individual property wrong, which is exactly the failure mode a like-for-like comparison exists to prevent. The largest quartile of properties is where this is most visible.
4. **The interval is well calibrated, and it buys that with width.** Coverage of the published range is **93.5%** with comparables and **95.3%** without, against point-estimate PE25 of **78.1%** and **65.5%**. Coverage that high should not be read as accuracy: the median half-width is **±52%** with comparables and **±60%** without. `rangeHalfWidth` never publishes a band narrower than the 20% market threshold and multiplies it by up to three as comparables run out, so a subject with no comparable is shown a range of ±60% — on a $180,000 property, $72,000 to $288,000. That is an honest statement of uncertainty and a nearly useless one for a pricing decision. The band narrows to ±28% at four comparables while coverage holds at 100%, which is where the interval starts to be worth reading.

5. **The ABOVE_MARKET verdict is very nearly unreachable, and the mechanism is structural.** Across all 387 subjects the flag is raised **1 time**. The cause is visible in the table above: `computeFlag` returns ABOVE_MARKET only for an asking price that is outside the published band but within `implausible_threshold_percent` of the estimate. That threshold is 60%, and `rangeHalfWidth` widens the band toward ±60% as comparables run out — so the two boundaries converge and the middle verdict is squeezed to nothing. At zero comparables the window is exactly zero percentage points wide. The two settings were chosen independently and interact in a way neither anticipates.

   Two honest qualifications. First, this sample is CLOSED properties, whose `asking_price` is the price they closed at, so it sits near the estimate by construction; the flag distribution here is not what a queue of live submissions would show. Second, the finding that survives that caveat is the geometric one, which does not depend on the sample at all: whenever the band half-width reaches the implausibility threshold, ABOVE_MARKET cannot be returned for any asking price whatsoever. The defect is in the interaction of two configuration values, and the fix is to derive the implausibility threshold from the band rather than to set it independently.
6. **Sample sparsity, not model form, is the binding constraint.** 59.9% of subjects have no comparable at all because only 387 properties in the seed are CLOSED, spread across 51 districts, several property types and two deal types, then narrowed again by the plus-or-minus 30% area band. The fix is more closed transactions, not a different estimator.
